## Context

openPractice is an assembly root whose code leg is empty. Constitution v1.0.0 (`.specify/memory/constitution.md` in `opensoft/openPractice`, ratified 2026-08-24) is in force. Its principles are I (HIPAA by design), II (security review), III (test-first), IV (clinical boundary), V (versioned contracts) and VI (portability). The framing decisions FD-1 to FD-11 were ratified on 2026-08-27. FD-5 is the governance primitive: four fixed tiers, approval kept only for tier 4, and "the second human does not exist; approval queues rot within a month."

Five OpenSpec changes are merged on `main` at `0a82fc2`, all `Status: draft` and unratified. Each fails closed at its own gates. The written critique of 2026-11-15, item C.1 ("Fail-Closed Cold-Start Paralysis"), says that together they leave a small practice unable to go live until four authorities have signed nearly a dozen artifacts, with no statement of how those roles map onto one physician-owner. Its recommended action: keep fail-closed runtime enforcement, add one readiness checklist and a policy starter kit, and define the role mapping for one person in several roles.

The owner ruled on that finding on 2026-11-15 (ruling 107, below). This change encodes ruling 107, and rulings 139, 140, 147 and 149, which the owner made for it on the same day. Rulings 136 to 138, 141 to 146, 148 and 150, and rulings 161, 164, 165, 184, 185 and 191 to 193, are encoded in the corrections change (PR #8), and this change follows them there. Where it goes further than any ruling, the step is a design call marked PROPOSED. **Rulings are not ratification.** This change stays `Status: draft`, and `owner-ratification.md` stays PENDING until the owner completes it.

Requirement IDs `RC-nn` (`readiness-checklist`), `SK-nn` (`policy-starter-kit`) and `SR-nn` (`signer-roles`) prefix every requirement name in the three spec deltas. Requirement IDs of other changes are cited as those changes define them; a requirement of `add-reimbursement-retention-control` or `define-customer-financial-data-boundaries`, which carry no IDs, is cited by its name. Requirements that exist only on an open pull request are cited by ID and described, not quoted.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals**

- One place that shows every go-live gate and policy sign-off the other changes require, with its enforcing requirement, authority, holder, state and consequence.
- A starter template for each policy a gate needs, inert until signed, and signed exactly like a policy written from scratch.
- Ruling 107 encoded: several roles per person, the role on every sign-off, one-person alternatives for two-person rules, and no self-approved accountant or counsel sign-off.

**Non-Goals**

- The text of any policy, template, agreement or consent form, and any legal, accounting or clinical rule.
- Weakening a gate, adding a gate, or changing who signs a gate, beyond ruling 107's role record and self-approval bar. SR-04 adds no record and no precondition outside the sign-offs it names, except what rulings 139, 140 and 149 order, and the owner-link precondition and the attestation's confirmation of the owners that RD-11 proposes, all disclosed as T-9.
- Editing another change. Every amendment below is proposed for its owner.
- A new contract, or any change to an existing contract.

## Decisions

### 1. One checklist that reads the gates and never stands in for them

A readiness item is recorded once per practice, counterparty, destination, agent, roster entry or version, and is either a precondition of a function under an existing requirement, or a version that a requirement gives a named authority to approve while a fail-closed default runs meanwhile, such as the routing-clock policy (design call RD-1). A sign-off made once per transaction, such as a batch release or a link confirmation, is a work item in the inbox (WI-07) and is not a row. Items the product owner decides at release, such as the grant sets of the new recipes, are shown read-only in their own section (RD-2). This change names no single go-live event of its own: each row says what stays off until it is done (RC-05). For ruling 161's set-up window it adopts the reading of PR #8's design call DC-69 (PROPOSED there): the practice goes live at the earliest of its first party record of any role, such as a patient, a guarantor or a contact, the first gateway capability enabled, the first accounting destination live and the first agent grant.

Each row reads its state from the gate's own record (RC-03), and shows `unavailable` when that record cannot be read (RC-04). The checklist keeps no state that a gate consults, offers no write path except the gate's own action, and enables nothing. Its rows follow the requirements (RC-02): the inventory below is the starting set, a later change that alters a gate alters its row, a ruling that no requirement encodes produces no row, and a consequence found only in a design, task list or open question is never shown.

### 2. A starter kit of inert, versioned templates

The kit holds one template for each policy, profile or plan a readiness gate needs (SK-01): fifteen. A template is never in force (SK-02). A practice copies it into its own draft, and the authority the gate names approves that draft through the gate's own action, with the same record as a policy written from scratch (SK-03). The version records the template it came from as provenance only (RD-6). Nothing is adopted by default or in bulk (SK-04). A published template version never changes, and a newer one never touches a policy in force (SK-05). Templates assert no rule and may show only values a requirement already states (SK-06, RD-7); the autonomy opt-in set shows AO-04's caps, never a suggested rung. openPractice's product team drafts each template; counsel, an accountant and a physician review it before release; and each practice's own authority still adopts each policy (ruling 147; SK-06, RD-20).

### 3. Roles, and the two-person rules

A role is a recipe, an actor type, or a capacity a requirement names (RD-9). Where a requirement says "an authorized person" and names no other role, that person is the practice manager, and the record keeps the role used (ruling 142). One person may hold several, each sign-off records the one role it was given in, and no authority comes from a combination of roles (SR-01, SR-02, RD-10, RD-17, RD-18). A rule that needs two different people fails closed until its owning change names a one-person alternative, and whenever the alternative it names is unavailable (SR-03). A rule that only loses its meaning when one person signs both sides keeps working, with every role recorded; the checklist lists the case, or counts it where its subject is a patient-side record (SR-03, RC-06, RD-19).

The accountant's sign-offs, and the counsel sign-offs that a requirement records, are never self-approved, and no one-person alternative applies to them (SR-04). The rulings set the test. The accountant's sign-off comes only from someone engaged from outside the practice who is neither an owner nor an employee, never from an employed accountant in version 1 (ruling 139); that accountant is a licensed CPA whose licence is checked against the state board's public record, and who attests in their own name (ruling 140); and the accountant's roster entry holds the outside-accountant recipe and nothing else (ruling 141). The accountant is matched against the practice's staff and owners, a match refusing the sign-off (ruling 139, read with ruling 149's question, "as the accountant is"). A named counsel is checked against the state bar's public record and matched against the practice's staff and owners, as the accountant is (ruling 149). The details beyond the rulings' words, including the method of matching, the attestation's confirmation of the recorded owners and an owner-link precondition, are design call RD-11, and all of it is disclosed as T-9. SR-04 reaches only PH-03's and PG-06's recorded counsel reviews, AH-06's recorded confirmation under ruling 102, AH-02's recorded confirmation of a provider's cache under rulings 133 and 138, and the accountant's sign-offs, which will include the material write-off amount that ruling 141 calls the write-off threshold once `define-revenue-cycle-core` lands (ruling 129). Where a requirement only describes counsel's part (AH-02's sentence on each agreement's form, PG-15, PG-16, and AH-06's sentence on the consent's form), it records no counsel sign-off, and SR-04 adds nothing there.

The one-person alternatives below are chosen in this order of preference (RD-13): another eligible named human, where the roster has one; an independent external record, as ruling 98 uses the board's public record; and, where neither exists, the sign-off given in both roles, each recorded, with the checklist listing or counting it.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **[The checklist is mistaken for a gate]** → It has no write path and enables nothing (RC-03), and shows `unavailable` rather than a stale state (RC-04); tests prove it.
- **[The checklist drifts from the requirements]** → Rows derive from requirements and follow them (RC-02); a ruling with no requirement has no row, and a consequence from a design or task list is never shown.
- **[The checklist reveals that a staff member is a patient]** → A use whose subject is a patient-side record is only counted, and the count and its opaque reference are shown only to readers authorized to see that PHI (RC-06, RD-19).
- **[A template is treated as approved policy]** → Templates are never read by a gate (SK-02); approval is the gate's own act (SK-03); nothing is adopted by default or in bulk (SK-04); the reviews before release approve nothing for any practice (ruling 147).
- **[Templates give legal or clinical comfort they cannot give]** → Each carries a statement that it asserts no rule (SK-06), and counsel, an accountant and a physician review it before release (ruling 147).
- **[One person signs everything without anyone noticing]** → Every sign-off records its role (SR-02); the checklist shows each person's roles and lists or counts every case where one person signed both sides (RC-06).
- **[A solo practice cannot find an accountant or counsel]** → Rulings 107, 139 and 140 accept that cost for the sign-offs SR-04 names; SR-04 says so plainly, adds no counsel sign-off elsewhere, and the checklist shows the need.
- **[An owner clears themselves as the outside accountant or as counsel]** → The accountant attests in their own name and the CPA licence is checked against the state board's public record (ruling 140); a named counsel is checked against the state bar's public record (ruling 149); each is matched against the practice's staff and owners (rulings 139 and 149; the method is RD-11); and the accountant's attestation confirms the recorded owners, and the accountant's entry is created with its vendor arrangement, never relabeled (RD-11).
- **[An owner is not recorded, so the owner match finds nothing]** → Under RD-11 no accountant or counsel sign-off is accepted while no owner link is recorded (RI-56). The practice manager records owner links, and the first the practice ever records is never held for a second person or an outside accountant, so this needs no outside accountant (PR-15, F-12; T-9).
- **[The owner list behind the matches is incomplete]** → The precondition needs only one owner link, so a practice manager could record one real owner and leave out another, such as an owner who is a lawyer. Where an outside accountant is engaged, their attestation confirms the owners recorded, PR #8 notifies them of every owner-link change (F-12), and under RD-11 (PROPOSED) ending or changing an owner link reopens the attestation, and the checklist shows the reopened attestation as the reason the accountant's rows wait (RC-06). Ending a link clears no one for 24 months, because the matches count a link that ended within that time, except one ended as recorded in error (RD-11). Where no accountant is engaged, nobody outside the practice confirms the owner list behind counsel's match: it rests on the practice manager's record, audited and notified to the other managers. This residual is accepted, not closed.
- **[Owners gain a power through the links]** → This change gives owners none (T-9), and ruling 193 rules that a recorded owner grants nothing by itself.
- **[A former owner or employee acts as accountant or counsel soon after leaving]** → The matches count an owner link or staff roster entry that ended within the last 24 months, from the later of its end date and the time the ending was recorded (RD-11, PROPOSED, put to the owner); after that a former owner or employee may serve, alike. An owner link ended as `recorded_in_error` does not count: that ending is decided by another holder of the practice manager or the controller or office manager recipe, or under the one-person alternative where there is none (PR #8's F-12, PROPOSED), and it reopens the attestation; where no accountant is engaged, nobody outside the practice checks it.
- **[Many amendments for other owners]** → The required amendments are in PR #8: five adopted under rulings, and RA-5a to RA-5c as F-7's extension, PROPOSED there. Six conforming amendments are applied there; they restate what SR-02 and SR-04 already bind, and CA-5 to CA-8 apply only if their owners come to record counsel's part.
